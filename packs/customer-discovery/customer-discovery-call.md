## Objective

Leave the call genuinely understanding whether this person has a real, frequent,
expensive problem you could solve — grounded in what they *actually did* recently, not
what they say they'd do — and secure a concrete next step that costs them something
(time, referral, money, or reputation). You are here to learn, not to pitch.

**Success looks like:**
- You can state their top problem in *their* words, with a specific recent example.
- You know how often it happens and what it costs them (time / money / risk).
- You know what they do about it today and what they've already tried or paid for.
- You got a real commitment (a next meeting, an intro, access, a pilot, or a pre-order),
  not a compliment.
- You did most of the listening.

## Principles

- **Do:** Talk about their life and past behavior, not your idea.
- **Do:** Ask about specific, recent events ("walk me through the last time…").
- **Do:** Dig for the cost and workaround of the problem before assuming it matters.
- **Do:** Shut up. Aim to listen ~80% of the time; let silence pull out the real answer.
- **Don't:** Pitch, demo, or mention your solution until discovery is genuinely done.
- **Don't:** Ask hypotheticals ("would you use…", "how much would you pay for…") — the
  answers are worthless.
- **Don't:** Fish for compliments or accept them as signal. "That's cool" is not data.
- **Don't:** Lead the witness. Ask open questions and let them go where it hurts.

## Steps

### 1. Set the frame

- **Goal:** Lower the stakes so they're candid, and earn permission to dig.
- **Ask:**
  - "Thanks for the time. I'm trying to learn, not sell you anything — is it okay if I mostly ask about how you work today?"
  - "To set context, tell me a bit about your role and what a normal week looks like."
- **Avoid:**
  - Opening with your idea or company.
  - Framing it as a sales call.
- **Advance when:** They've relaxed and given you a picture of their role and week.

### 2. Understand their world

- **Goal:** Map their context, goals, and where this problem might live.
- **Ask:**
  - "What are you actually responsible for / measured on?"
  - "Where does most of your time go in a week?"
  - "What's the hardest or most annoying part of that?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Jumping to the problem you *hope* they have.
- **Advance when:** They've named a friction point or annoyance unprompted.

### 3. Get to the real problem

- **Goal:** Find a concrete, recent instance of the pain — not a general complaint.
- **Ask:**
  - "Walk me through the last time that happened. What did you actually do?"
  - "Who else was involved? What broke down?"
  - "How often does that come up?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Accepting vague generalities ("it's always a mess") without a specific example.
  - Suggesting the answer.
- **Advance when:** You have a specific, recent story of the problem with real detail.

### 4. Quantify the pain

- **Goal:** Learn whether this is a vitamin or a painkiller — frequency and cost.
- **Ask:**
  - "How much time / money does that cost you when it happens?"
  - "What's the downstream impact when it goes wrong?"
  - "On a bad week, how many times does this hit you?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Letting them hand-wave the cost; get to numbers or a concrete consequence.
- **Advance when:** You can estimate the frequency and the cost of the problem.

### 5. Map current solutions

- **Goal:** Understand the status quo you'd have to beat, and what they'll pay for.
- **Ask:**
  - "What do you do about it today?"
  - "What have you already tried, hacked together, or bought?"
  - "Where does that fall short?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Assuming there's no existing solution. There always is — even if it's a spreadsheet.
- **Advance when:** You know their current workaround and where it fails.

### 6. Test for pull

- **Goal:** Find out if they're actively trying to solve this — the only real demand signal.
- **Ask:**
  - "Have you looked for something better? What happened?"
  - "Is fixing this on anyone's roadmap or budget?"
  - "If this were solved, what would change for you?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Asking if they'd *like* a solution (everyone says yes). Ask what they've *done*.
- **Advance when:** You can tell whether they're searching for a fix or just venting.

### 7. Secure the commitment

- **Goal:** Convert interest into a next step that costs them something real.
- **Ask:**
  - "Would it make sense to [try a pilot / intro me to whoever owns budget / grab 30 min next week to go deeper]?"
  - "Who else on your team should I talk to?"
  - "Can I follow up when I have something to show you?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Ending on "I'll keep you posted" with no ask.
  - Treating "sounds great" as a commitment — get a date, an intro, or access.
- **Advance when:** You have a concrete commitment or a clear, honest no.

### 8. Wrap and capture

- **Goal:** Close warmly and lock in the structured takeaways while they're fresh.
- **Ask:**
  - "This was really helpful — anything I should have asked but didn't?"
  - "Mind if I come back to you as this develops?"
- **Avoid:**
  - Letting the insights evaporate after the call.
- **Advance when:** The call is wrapped and the Outputs below are filled in.

## Signals

- **Vague generality:** They describe the problem in the abstract → push for the last specific instance before believing it matters.
- **Compliment:** "That's a cool idea" / "I'd totally use that" → discount it; steer back to what they actually do today.
- **Active search:** They've already tried tools, built a hack, or have budget → strong pull; move toward a commitment.
- **Workaround is fine:** Their current fix works well enough → the pain may be too small; probe cost harder or disqualify.
- **Emotional spike:** Frustration, a story with heat behind it → follow it; that's where the real pain lives.
- **You're talking too much:** If you've said more than they have, stop and ask an open question.

## Outputs

- [ ] Problem statement in their words, with a specific recent example
- [ ] Frequency of the problem (how often it happens)
- [ ] Cost of the problem (time / money / risk when it goes wrong)
- [ ] Current workaround and where it falls short
- [ ] Pull signal: are they actively trying to solve this? (evidence, not opinion)
- [ ] Commitment secured (next meeting / intro / access / pilot / pre-order) — or a clear no
- [ ] Follow-ups and other people to talk to
- [ ] One-line verdict: is this a real, frequent, expensive problem worth pursuing?
